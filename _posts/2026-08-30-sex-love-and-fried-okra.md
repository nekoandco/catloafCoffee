She used to be a rare sight, but now I see her every day. So does everyone else. No one really sees a boy anymore. It’s nice. 

I used to think of my current self and my pre-transition self as one and the same, but these days I see them more as separate entities. While my body has changed, so has my mind. I can’t see myself as a man any longer, even if I try. Likewise, I don’t think about being trans all the time as I once did, because it is an increasingly irrelevant detail in everyday life. 

I’m just a girl.

I always assumed it would take years to reach passing status, if I ever did. But one year into my transition and I realized I was passing, forcing me to reckon with what was next. I came to understand that while the success of my transition felt good, it also felt hollow. I was lonelier than ever: isolated from most of my family, with a lack of friends, or anyone who understands what life is like as a trans woman. Essentially, my new body and mind didn’t mean anything without anyone to share it with.

Then I met Ruby and everything started to fall into place. Suddenly, all the pain and heartache I had undergone to realize my true self was worth it. Ruby understands me: the Autistic side of me, the trans side of me, and the uniquely Sage side of me. She’s my person. Life feels wondrous and exciting with her.

My phone rings: a FaceTime from Mom. I answer and tell her Ruby will join us soon. Mom is excited; it’ll be her first time meeting Ruby. Despite having only mentioned Ruby offhand to her a couple of times, Mom somehow recognized Ruby’s importance to me within our first month together. I was nervous she wouldn’t take us seriously given the relatively short amount of time we’ve been together, but she reassured me, saying, “When you know, you know.” Mother’s intuition, I suppose.

We chat about our week for a few minutes, and I spot Ruby enter the shop’s doors. She sits next to me, takes a sip of my coffee, and says hello to Mom. She shows us the things she bought at the store—hooks, lures, shiny things—and tells Mom about her birthday, flashing her whale earrings proudly.

The call goes well. Mom says Ruby and I are cute together before ending the call. I look over to Ruby, who is smiling at me. There’s so much love in that smile.

“Let’s get out here,” she says.

I take her hand, and she leads me from the coffee shop to the harbor nearby. A well-kept, urban park runs along the shoreline, and a few scattered couples have tucked themselves in little nooks. Ruby takes me to the harbor’s edge, right up to the railing, the sea waters quietly sloshing a few meters below. She takes my cheek in her hand, looks into my eyes, then pulls me close and kisses me.

We stay there by the railing for a while, holding each other close. Watching the harbor’s waves glimmer in the moonlight. Sharing silence. Enjoying the calm together. In this moment, I am completely at peace.

My head resting on her shoulder and her hand massaging my arm, I start to speak just as Ruby does the same.

In perfect unison: “I love you.”

Giggling, we begin walking back home, both of us relieved at finally having found someone to do life with.
